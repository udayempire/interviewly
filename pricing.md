# Interviewlyy pricing strategy: 2026 market research and unit economics

**Research snapshot:** October 2026, based on public pricing pages and provider documentation available during this research. Prices change frequently; recheck provider and competitor pages before publishing prices. Currency conversions and cost scenarios below are estimates, not provider quotes.

## Executive recommendation

Launch with a free plan and three paid monthly plans. Meter included **interview minutes**, while also publishing an understandable maximum duration for one session. Display approximate interview counts as examples, because actual cost follows minutes used and session/report activity rather than an abstract interview count.

| Plan | Price in India | International price | Included minutes/month | Example maximum sessions | Max duration/session |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Free | ₹0 | $0 | 15 | 1 | 15 min |
| Starter | ₹599/month | $7.99/month | 60 | 2 | 30 min |
| Pro | ₹1,499/month | $19.99/month | 180 | 4 | 45 min |
| Premium | ₹2,999/month | $39.99/month | 360 | 6 | 60 min |

Offer monthly billing and a discounted annual option only after validating retention. As an initial annual discount, 15–20% is enough; charge upfront and make the total billed amount prominent. Unused monthly minutes expire at the end of the billing period. Let users buy one-off minute packs after they hit their limit. Do not sell unlimited live voice sessions.

This structure aims for roughly **45–55% contribution margin under conservative full-use assumptions**, after modeled AI costs, a small variable infrastructure allowance, and payment fees. It has materially more protection than a 30% target while remaining below many premium interview products. India prices are purchasing-power-localized rather than mechanical USD conversion; validate conversion before treating these as final.

A few products are unlimited, but their public pages often do not disclose maximum session durations or actual fair-use limits.

### Employer-side AI interview products (adjacent market)

**Hyring AI Interviewer** is primarily a recruiting product, not a self-serve candidate-practice substitute. G2’s 2026 pricing listing reports tiers such as $99 for 20 credits, $299 for 70 credits, and larger plans. Its Lite listing includes a 10-minute interview duration. [G2 pricing listing](https://www.g2.com/products/ai-interviewer/pricing)

### Competitor takeaways

1. Free access is common, but it ranges from a few questions or lifetime sessions to broad free access. Free access is a user-acquisition instrument, not a consistent category standard.
2. Consumer candidates see subscriptions roughly from $8–$28/month for recurring practice, while unlimited offerings are sometimes available for $20–$44/month. One-off technical packs can be $10 for five long mocks. These are advertised terms; “unlimited” may have undisclosed platform fair-use rules.
3. Many pages omit duration caps. EngMock is a clear long-form technical practice reference: one credit equals a 45- or 60-minute interview. Yoodli offers configurable roleplay limits for organizations. This makes explicit minute caps an opportunity to build trust.
4. India-focused candidates can find one-time or localized plans in the low hundreds of rupees, while more extensive prep bundles can be higher. Do not assume that US prices convert directly into acceptable India prices.
5. Resume/job-description personalization, voice practice, immediate feedback and company/role-specific scenarios are differentiators users can understand. Interviewlyy should package value around those capabilities, not merely “AI minutes.”

**Fallback rule:** use cross-provider fallbacks for transient errors, timeouts and rate limits, not for every response. A successful response from a fallback should be tagged in telemetry. A user’s invalid BYOK key should fall back to platform credentials only if product policy permits it and the user understands that behavior. Free-tier vendor APIs should not be treated as production fallbacks; rate limits can fail in correlated bursts.

## 3. Cost assumptions and API cost estimates

The following estimates model a **split voice stack**: STT + text LLM + TTS + one final feedback/report call. They are not quotes from a single provider and should be replaced with measured token and audio usage.

### Usage assumptions

**Realistic case** (normal English interview):

- GPT‑5.6 Terra at $2/M input and $12/M output tokens.
- Average 2,000 billed input tokens and 120 output tokens per wall-clock interview minute. Input includes conversational context and prompt overhead, but assumes prompt caching/context management keeps growth moderate.
- Streaming STT at $0.0077/min, a non-promotional reference rate from Deepgram’s listed standard price; current pricing page displays some lower promotional rates.
- AI interviewer speaks around 20% of interview time at approximately 130 words/minute, approximately 156 generated characters per wall-clock minute. At Deepgram Aura‑2 $0.03/1,000 chars, this is about $0.0047/min.
- Final evaluation/report cost allowance: $0.02/session.
- No unusually high retry rate, image/audio vision input, premium TTS, or non-English model surcharge.

This produces about **$0.0209 per interview minute plus $0.02 per completed session** (rounded to $0.021/minute + $0.02/session).

**Conservative case** (long context, more model output, multilingual/premium voice):

- 8,000 input tokens and 400 output tokens per wall-clock minute at GPT‑5.6 Terra rates (LLM about $0.0208/minute).
- STT allowance $0.0092/minute (multilingual / standard-rate stress).
- Multilingual TTS at $0.10/1,000 characters and the same interviewer speech share (about $0.0156/minute).
- $0.03/session for final report and retries.

This produces about **$0.0456/minute plus $0.03/session** (rounded to $0.046/minute + $0.03/session). It is intentionally conservative, not an expected median.

### Estimated cost per interview length

| Interview length | Realistic API cost | Conservative API cost | Approx. ₹ realistic / conservative at ₹95 per $1 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 min | $0.23 | $0.49 | ₹22 / ₹47 |
| 20 min | $0.44 | $0.95 | ₹42 / ₹90 |
| 30 min | $0.65 | $1.41 | ₹62 / ₹134 |
| 45 min | $0.97 | $2.10 | ₹92 / ₹200 |
| 60 min | $1.28 | $2.79 | ₹122 / ₹265 |

**Currency assumption:** ₹95 per US dollar is used as a rounded planning conversion only; actual conversion and exchange spreads vary. A March 2026 FEDAI revaluation sheet listed USD near ₹95, but that is not an October spot quote. Refresh before committing to foreign-currency budgets. [FEDAI rates, March 30, 2026](https://fedai.org.in/DocumentUploadFiles/RevaluationRates/RevaluationRates_30_03_2026.pdf)

### Cost factors that could exceed this model

- Conversation history resent on every turn can make input token usage grow with interview length; context truncation or prompt caching changes the curve.
- Code and system-design sessions may contain much larger context or structured artifacts.
- More interviewer speech increases TTS bill; premium TTS may be 2× the chosen TTS rate.
- Failed requests that time out after the provider processed them can still be billable. Blind retries may double charge.
- Report generation may be a large one-shot prompt and should be included in completed-session cost.
- Multi-language transcription, diarization, keyterm prompting, storage, recording retention and moderation can incur more fees.
- Provider failover can cost extra if both primary and fallback request complete.

## 4. Free allowance: acquisition versus cost

### Options compared

Assume a free session lasts the full length and uses realistic API cost, $0.021/min + $0.02/report. Also show the conservative cost, $0.046/min + $0.03/report.

| Free offer | API cost/user/month, realistic | Conservative API cost | Acquisition / abuse tradeoff |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 × 10 min/month | $0.23 | $0.49 | Good low-risk trial but may be too short to show adaptive follow-ups and a useful report. |
| 1 × 15 min/month | $0.34 | $0.72 | Recommended. Enough to demonstrate voice turn-taking, personalization and feedback; predictable maximum cost. |
| 2 × 15 min/month | $0.67 | $1.41 | Better repeat use, doubles max cost. Use only if free-to-paid conversion supports it. |
| 3 × 15 min/month | $1.01 | $2.19 | High ongoing subsidy, enough for several short practice rounds; attractive acquisition lever but prone to multi-account abuse. |
| 5 × 15 min/month | $1.69 | $3.60 | Expensive for inactive/nonconverting users; not advisable as an always-on default. |
| 1 × 30 min/month | $0.65 | $1.41 | Strong experience, costs about twice the recommended trial; consider an occasional onboarding campaign. |

### Recommendation

Offer **one 15-minute voice mock per calendar month**. This gives a product demonstration that includes several turns and personalized response without offering a steady stream of free practice. A one-time initial trial of 20–25 minutes could improve first-session comprehension, then switch to the 15-minute monthly free allowance; test rather than assume it converts better.

The free experience should include a useful but bounded report, not artificially degrade voice quality so much that users cannot evaluate the product. Control abuse with per-account/per-device rate limits, email/phone verification where justified, concurrent-session limits, request duration limits and automated anomaly detection. Set a global daily free-spend circuit breaker for provider outages/abuse. Do not rely on a provider’s free API tier as the economic plan: quotas and terms are not guaranteed and can change.

There is no public competitor data that establishes a causal free-to-paid conversion rate for this product category. Therefore, the acquisition/conversion tradeoff is a hypothesis to measure with cohorts: cost per activated free user, percent finishing a session, percent viewing a report, and paid conversion within 7/30 days. Keep the free cap conservative until those numbers justify a higher subsidy.

## 5. Duration and expected user behavior

Competitor evidence is mixed. EngMock explicitly packages 45- or 60-minute full technical interviews. Interviewing.io’s AI interviewer is free but does not publish a universal time cap. Yoodli’s roleplays can have configurable time limits; its individual paid plan counts sessions or markets unlimited rather than a minutes balance. Other interview platforms commonly publish session/credit allowances but omit hard durations.

Candidate expectations likely differ by purpose:

- **Quick drill:** 5–15 minutes for one question, answer practice, or a behavioral warm-up.
- **Focused mock:** 20–30 minutes for a focused behavioral or technical practice loop.
- **Full-length round:** 45–60 minutes for a realistic technical/case round, especially coding and system design.
- **Multiple rounds:** candidates often prepare over multiple separate sessions, not one extremely long call; separate sessions make progress and feedback easier to review.

These are product-use assumptions inferred from competitor formats and common interview formats, not a measured survey of Interviewlyy users.

### Recommended session durations

- Free: 15 minutes maximum.
- Starter: 30 minutes maximum/session.
- Pro: 45 minutes maximum/session.
- Premium: 60 minutes maximum/session.
- Absolute platform maximum: **60 minutes**, with a warning at 5 minutes remaining and a clear save/finish step.

Avoid unlimited duration. A maximum protects cost, defines an interview format, and creates a graceful end point for summaries and reports. Users wanting two rounds can start a second session using their remaining minutes.

### Cost/margin method

For economics, use the conservative API cost **$0.046/minute + $0.03 per completed session**, the full monthly minutes above, and a variable infrastructure/support allowance of **$0.40 per paid account-month**. Payment fee assumptions are **2.36% India domestic** (Razorpay lists 2% + 18% GST on the fee for most domestic transactions) and **3.5% international blended** as a planning assumption, not a universal gateway quote. Actual fees vary by payment method, tax and merchant agreement. [Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)

“Contribution margin” below means `(subscription revenue - AI/API cost - variable infra allowance - payment fee) / subscription revenue`. It is not accounting gross margin; it excludes fixed salaries, marketing, tax on sales, refunds, chargebacks, support labor and overhead. India’s listed plan prices are treated as pre-GST catalog prices for this comparison; if prices shown to consumers include GST, adjust net revenue accordingly.

| Plan | Full-allowance conservative API cost | Approx. API cost in INR at ₹95/$ | Modeled full-use contribution margin | Realistic API cost at full use | Average API cost at 55% use (realistic) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Free | $0.72 | ₹68 | n/a | $0.34 | $0.19 at 55% of allowance |
| Starter | $2.88 | ₹274 | ~48% India / ~54% international | $1.30 | $0.72 |
| Pro | $8.46 | ₹804 | ~40% India / ~49% international | $3.82 | $2.11 |
| Premium | $16.86 | ₹1,602 | ~44% India / ~49% international | $7.72 | $4.26 |

Calculations include the $0.40 variable-cost reserve and payment fee. Rounded figures. Free API at full 15 minutes uses $0.046 × 15 + $0.03 = $0.72; realistic is $0.021 × 15 + $0.02 = $0.34.

The Pro plan’s full-use India margin is the weakest at these prices. If users routinely use all 180 minutes, consider either increasing it to ₹1,699, reducing allowance to 150 minutes, or promoting annual instead of monthly. Do not hide a lower actual allowance behind “unlimited.”

### Expected average use

Expected margins depend on usage. For this table, usage means percent of included minutes consumed; session/report overhead is estimated in proportion to usage. The variable infrastructure allowance is still charged at $0.40/month. Plan prices use ₹95/$ for comparison.

| Plan | Light (25% allowance) realistic API cost | Average (55%) realistic API cost | Heavy (100%) realistic API cost | Average-use modeled contribution margin India / international |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Starter | ~$0.33 | ~$0.72 | ~$1.30 | ~83% / ~86% |
| Pro | ~$0.96 | ~$2.11 | ~$3.82 | ~81% / ~86% |
| Premium | ~$1.93 | ~$4.26 | ~$7.72 | ~82% / ~86% |

Those high average-use margins are not guaranteed profit; fixed costs, acquisition, churn, refunds and user support still matter. They demonstrate that plans can tolerate average usage materially below the hard ceiling, while the full-use conservative row is the important stress guardrail.

### Full-use and API-price shocks

Assume all included minutes are consumed and use conservative baseline cost ($0.046/min + $0.03/session). A 25% or 50% provider price increase is applied to the AI/API portion, not to payment fees or infrastructure.

| Plan | Baseline max API cost | API cost +25% | API cost +50% | Contribution margin at +50%: India / international |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Starter | $2.88 | $3.60 | $4.32 | ~24% / ~36% |
| Pro | $8.46 | $10.58 | $12.69 | ~11% / ~28% |
| Premium | $16.86 | $21.08 | $25.29 | ~15% / ~32% |

At full utilization, a 50% price increase erodes margins sharply, especially for Pro. This is why the recommendation is a conservative usage cap, modest premium plan repricing headroom, multi-provider cost routing, and quarterly cost review. An API price change of +50% should trigger a price/allowance decision, not be absorbed indefinitely.

### Longer average sessions

All minute plans bound session duration but can still be consumed through many long sessions. If average session length rises while total minutes stay capped, API cost remains approximately bounded by minutes but per-session report overhead goes down. If customers use the same number of interviews but each becomes longer, total API cost rises proportionally. For example, 4 Pro sessions averaging 45 minutes consume all 180 minutes; increasing each to 60 minutes would need 240 minutes and cost about 33% more, so the plan must either stop at its 180-minute allowance or require a top-up.

## 8. India versus international pricing

### What the evidence supports

- OpenAI’s consumer subscription billing supports localized currencies, including INR, to reduce conversion friction; this is an example of localized checkout, not proof every SaaS should apply a particular PPP discount. [OpenAI multi-currency billing](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10421635-multicurrency-billing)
- India-focused interview competitors explicitly display prices in rupees: CareerLaunch AI lists a ₹299 monthly option and ₹1,499 annual option; ClavePrep shows localized interview/credit prices; PrepVisor lists one-time passes in the ₹199–₹499 range. These products and allowances differ, so they are market anchors rather than direct apples-to-apples equivalents.
- Competitor rates often differ by whether they sell question practice, 15-session toolkits, full voice interviews, live interview assistance or a bundle of job-search tools. India price points should be segmented by value and willingness-to-pay, not merely copied.

### Recommendation

Use **localized INR and USD catalog pricing**. For India, use ₹599 / ₹1,499 / ₹2,999; internationally use $7.99 / $19.99 / $39.99. India prices are materially below straightforward USD conversion at ₹95/$, reflecting candidate purchasing power and local competition. The Premium India price is still higher than small practice subscriptions, justified only if full-length, resume/GitHub-personalized interviews and high-quality reports are demonstrably valuable.

Apply region localization using billing country/payment instrument and local tax rules, not a user-editable currency toggle alone. Avoid arbitrary country restrictions: offer scholarship/discount codes for students/job seekers if data supports it. Monitor currency movements; if rupee depreciation or model costs push effective margin below the floor, review INR prices quarterly. Show GST treatment clearly. Avoid annual lock-in until refunds, cancellation and renewal disclosures are clear.

## 9. Top-ups, rollover and failure treatment

These are commercial policy recommendations, not claims about every competitor.

| Situation | Recommended billing/allowance treatment |
|---|---|
| User starts, then leaves immediately | No full interview charge. Provide a 2-minute setup/grace window; then bill only active interview minutes. If no candidate speech or no substantive AI exchange occurs, restore minutes automatically. |
| Accidental start | Allow a short cancellation window (e.g. 2 minutes) to restore all minutes, once per billing period or account. Make start confirmation clear. |
| Early termination | Deduct actual billable minutes used, rounded to the nearest 30 seconds or minute; preserve the transcript/report if available. Do not consume a whole “interview credit.” |
| Candidate internet drops | Pause the billing clock after a short reconnect grace period (e.g. 90 seconds); hold session state for 5–10 minutes. Resume same session without charging setup again. If no resume is possible, refund unused allowance automatically. |
| Provider/model failure | Pause meter while the platform retries/fails over. If no usable response, refund time from last successful turn, keep completed transcript, and let candidate resume. Clearly distinguish provider failure from user disconnect. |
| Restart request | Resume same interview without resetting its allowance. If the user deliberately starts over, charge only minutes actually consumed on the abandoned attempt; do not issue a fresh full allotment automatically. Permit one no-cost restart if the platform caused the failure. |
| User ends early | Keep only used minutes and generate a partial report where there is enough transcript. Explain if the sample is too short for reliable scoring. |
| Allowance exhausted | Stop before the next paid call, save session, and offer a one-time top-up or plan upgrade. Never silently charge a stored payment method for overage without opt-in. |
| Unused monthly minutes | No rollover on monthly subscriptions; state this before purchase. Consider a limited rollover (up to 25% for one month) for annual plans as a retention benefit only after measuring liability. |
| One-time packs | No expiry or a long, conspicuous expiry (e.g. 12 months). Packs can be bought when usage is seasonal. Do not make credit expiry a surprise. |
| Refunds | Refund unused subscription period per the published policy and applicable law; service-side failures should restore minutes even when monetary refunds are not requested. Track chargebacks separately from API margin. |

### Suggested top-up menu

- 30-minute pack: ₹249 / $3.99.
- 90-minute pack: ₹599 / $8.99.
- 180-minute pack: ₹999 / $15.99.

Packs can have a lower per-minute rate at higher volume, but preserve margin under the conservative cost case. Optional credits can map to minutes: **one credit = 15 interview minutes**. Do not mix “credits,” “sessions” and “minutes” without a clear conversion on the pricing page.

## 10. Final recommendation and decision answers

1. **How many free interviews?** One 15-minute voice interview every month, plus optionally a one-time 20–25 minute first-use trial during acquisition experiments.
2. **Should interviews have a time limit?** Yes. State both plan minutes and maximum session length before the user starts.
3. **Maximum interview duration?** 60 minutes; free is 15, Starter 30, Pro 45, Premium 60.
4. **How many interviews per paid tier?** Starter supports roughly 2 × 30-minute sessions; Pro 4 × 45; Premium 6 × 60. These are examples within 60/180/360 minutes, not separate hard interview-count quotas.
5. **India price?** ₹599 / ₹1,499 / ₹2,999 per month for Starter/Pro/Premium; evaluate ₹499/₹1,299/₹2,499 as a controlled launch experiment only if conversion is weak and costs/allowances remain viable.
6. **International price?** $7.99 / $19.99 / $39.99 per month.
7. **Unit: interviews, credits or minutes?** Minutes as the main allowance; per-session maximum as the guardrail. Offer optional 15-minute credits for one-time top-ups.
8. **Do unused interviews roll over?** Monthly subscription minutes should not roll over initially. If retention data supports it, test up to 25% rollover for annual plans for one month.
9. **Can users buy extra interviews?** Yes. Offer clearly priced minute packs and require opt-in; no silent overage billing.
10. **What protects margin and competitiveness?** Three localized tiers with finite minutes, 30–60-minute caps, one modest free experience, usage-aware provider routing, top-ups, failure refunds, and real cost telemetry. Target **at least 45–50% contribution margin at expected use and roughly 40% or better at full conservative use**. Revisit Pro India price/allowance first because it has the lowest conservative full-use margin.
